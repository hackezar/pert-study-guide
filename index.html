Lesson 1 · Number skills

Whole numbers and order of operations

When a problem has several operations, the order you do them in changes the answer. Everyone follows the same order so everyone gets the same result.

The order: PEMDAS

  1. Parentheses
  2. Exponents
  3. Multiply and Divide, left to right
  4. Add and Subtract, left to right
3 + 4 × (10 − 6)² ÷ 8
  1. Parentheses: 10 − 6 = 4, so it is 3 + 4 × 4² ÷ 8
  2. Exponent: 4² = 16, so it is 3 + 4 × 16 ÷ 8
  3. Multiply: 4 × 16 = 64, so it is 3 + 64 ÷ 8
  4. Divide: 64 ÷ 8 = 8
  5. Add last: 3 + 8 = 11

Watch out. Multiply and divide have the same rank, so work them left to right. 24 ÷ 4 × 2 is 6 × 2 = 12, not 24 ÷ 8 = 3.

Rounding and estimating

To round, look at the digit just to the right of the place you are rounding to. If it is 5 or more, round up. Otherwise round down.

Round 4,372 to the nearest hundred
  1. The hundreds digit is 3. The digit to its right is 7.
  2. 7 is 5 or more, so round up: 4,400

Test tip. Estimating knocks out wrong choices fast. 398 × 21 is close to 400 × 20 = 8,000, so the answer is near 8,000.

Lesson 2 · Number skills

Fractions

A fraction is a part of a whole. The bottom number says how many equal pieces the whole is cut into. The top says how many pieces you have.

Build a fraction

Simplifying

Divide the top and bottom by the same number until nothing else divides both.

Simplify 12/18
  1. Both divide by 6: 12 ÷ 6 = 2 and 18 ÷ 6 = 3
  2. Answer: 2/3

Adding and subtracting

The bottoms must match first. Find a number both bottoms divide into, rewrite each fraction, then add or subtract only the tops.

3/4 − 1/6
  1. Both 4 and 6 go into 12.
  2. Rewrite: 3/4 = 9/12 and 1/6 = 2/12
  3. Subtract the tops: 9/12 − 2/12 = 7/12

Watch out. Never add the bottoms. 1/2 + 1/3 is 5/6, not 2/5.

Multiplying and dividing

To multiply, go straight across: tops times tops, bottoms times bottoms. To divide, flip the second fraction and multiply.

3/4 ÷ 2/5
  1. Keep the first, flip the second: 3/4 × 5/2
  2. Multiply across: 15/8

Mixed numbers

Turn a mixed number into one fraction before doing anything else. Multiply the whole number by the bottom and add the top.

2 1/3 × 3/4
  1. 2 × 3 + 1 = 7, so 2 1/3 = 7/3
  2. Multiply across: 7/3 × 3/4 = 21/12
  3. Simplify by 3: 7/4
Lesson 3 · Number skills

Decimals and percents

Fractions, decimals and percents are three ways to write the same amount: 1/4 = 0.25 = 25%. Most problems get easier once you switch to the form that fits.

Switching forms

FromToHowExample
FractionDecimalDivide top by bottom3/8 = 0.375
DecimalPercentMove the dot 2 places right0.375 = 37.5%
PercentDecimalMove the dot 2 places left6% = 0.06

Worth memorizing: 1/2 = 50%, 1/4 = 25%, 3/4 = 75%, 1/5 = 20%, 1/10 = 10%, 1/3 ≈ 33.3%, 1/8 = 12.5%.

To compare numbers in different forms, turn them all into decimals. Then use the symbols: < means less than and > means greater than. The small, pointed end always aims at the smaller number, as in 0.375 < 0.4.

Decimal arithmetic

  • Add or subtract: line up the dots.
  • Multiply: ignore the dots, multiply, then count the digits after the dots in both numbers and give the answer that many.
2.4 × 0.3
  1. Ignore the dots: 24 × 3 = 72
  2. One digit after the dot in each number makes two in total: 0.72

Slide the percent

Percent change and discounts

Percent change is the change divided by the original amount.

A price rises from $40 to $50. What is the percent increase?
  1. Change: 50 − 40 = 10
  2. Divide by the original: 10 ÷ 40 = 0.25
  3. As a percent: 25%
A $60 rash guard is 25% off. What is the sale price?
  1. 25% off means you pay 75%.
  2. 0.75 × 60 = 45, so the price is $45

Watch out. Always divide by the starting amount, not the new one. Going from 40 to 50 is a 25% rise, but going from 50 back to 40 is only a 20% drop.

Lesson 4 · Number skills

Ratios, rates and proportions

A ratio compares two amounts. A proportion says two ratios are equal, and it lets you find a missing number.

Unit rates

A unit rate is the amount for one. Divide to get it. 150 miles in 3 hours is 150 ÷ 3 = 50 miles per hour.

Solving a proportion

Cross-multiply: multiply each top by the opposite bottom, then solve.

3/4 = x/20
  1. Cross-multiply: 4 · x = 3 · 20
  2. 4x = 60
  3. Divide by 4: x = 15

Proportion word problems

Write both ratios in the same order, such as dollars on top and items on the bottom.

6 spam musubi cost $9. How much do 10 cost?
  1. Set it up with dollars on top: 9/6 = x/10
  2. Cross-multiply: 6x = 90
  3. x = 15, so 10 musubi cost $15

Shortcut. Find the price of one first: 9 ÷ 6 = 1.50, then 1.50 × 10 = 15.

Splitting by a ratio

A reef count finds 30 animals, turtles to rays 2:3. How many rays?
  1. The ratio has 2 + 3 = 5 parts.
  2. Each part is 30 ÷ 5 = 6 animals.
  3. Rays are 3 parts: 3 × 6 = 18

Changing units

Multiply or divide by what one unit equals. 90 minutes is 90 ÷ 60 = 1.5 hours. At 60 miles per hour, 20 minutes is 20/60 = 1/3 of an hour, so you go 60 × 1/3 = 20 miles.

A ridge hike is 3 miles. How many feet is that? (1 mile = 5,280 feet)
  1. Feet are smaller than miles, so there will be more of them: multiply.
  2. 3 × 5280 = 15840 feet

Which way? Changing to a smaller unit, multiply. Changing to a bigger unit, divide. Then check that the answer makes sense: a hike should be thousands of feet, not a fraction of one.

Lesson 5 · PERT algebra

Negative numbers and expressions

An expression is math with letters in it and no equals sign, like 3x + 5. You cannot solve it, but you can simplify it or find its value.

Negative number rules

SituationRuleExample
Subtracting a negativeSame as adding5 − (−3) = 8
Negative × negativePositive(−4)(−3) = 12
Negative × positiveNegative(−4)(3) = −12
Absolute valueDistance from zero, never negative|−7| = 7

Finding the value

Replace the letter with the number, in parentheses, then follow the order of operations.

2x² − 3x + 1 when x = −2
  1. Replace x: 2(−2)² − 3(−2) + 1
  2. Square first: (−2)² = 4, so 2(4) − 3(−2) + 1
  3. Multiply: 8 + 6 + 1
  4. Add: 15

Watch out. (−2)² is 4, but −2² is −4. The parentheses decide whether the minus gets squared.

Combining like terms

Only terms with the same letter and exponent combine. Plain numbers combine with plain numbers.

3x + 5 + 2x − 8
  1. x terms: 3x + 2x = 5x
  2. Numbers: 5 − 8 = −3
  3. Answer: 5x − 3

Distributing

A number outside parentheses multiplies every term inside.

3(x + 4) − 2(x − 5)
  1. Distribute the 3: 3x + 12
  2. Distribute the −2 to both terms: −2x + 10
  3. Combine: 3x − 2x + 12 + 10 = x + 22

Watch out. The minus sign goes with the 2. −2 × −5 is +10, the most commonly missed step on this kind of question.

Lesson 6 · PERT algebra

Equations and inequalities

An equation is a balance scale. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other, and it stays balanced. The goal is always to get the letter alone.

Balance the scale

=

The steps, in order

  1. Distribute to clear any parentheses.
  2. Combine like terms on each side.
  3. Move the x terms to one side and the numbers to the other.
  4. Divide by the number on x.
3(x − 2) = x + 10
  1. Distribute: 3x − 6 = x + 10
  2. Subtract x from both sides: 2x − 6 = 10
  3. Add 6 to both sides: 2x = 16
  4. Divide by 2: x = 8
  5. Check: 3(8 − 2) = 18 and 8 + 10 = 18. It works.

Equations with fractions

Multiply every term by a number all the bottoms divide into. The fractions disappear.

x/2 + x/3 = 10
  1. Multiply every term by 6: 3x + 2x = 60
  2. Combine: 5x = 60
  3. Divide by 5: x = 12

Inequalities

Solve them exactly like equations, with one extra rule.

−2x + 3 < 11
  1. Subtract 3 from both sides: −2x < 8
  2. Divide by −2 and flip the sign: x > −4

Watch out. Flip the inequality sign whenever you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number. Adding or subtracting never flips it.

Formulas with several letters

To solve a formula for one letter, treat the other letters like numbers.

Solve P = 2l + 2w for w
  1. Subtract 2l from both sides: P − 2l = 2w
  2. Divide by 2: w = (P − 2l)/2

Word problems

A surf shop rents a board for $50 plus $40 per hour. The bill is $210. How many hours?
  1. Fixed amount plus rate times hours: 50 + 40h = 210
  2. Subtract 50: 40h = 160
  3. Divide by 40: h = 4
Lesson 7 · PERT algebra

Lines and graphs

Every straight line can be written as y = mx + b, and only two numbers matter: m and b.

  • m is the slope: how steep the line is. It means "up this much for every 1 step right."
  • b is the y-intercept: where the line crosses the up-and-down axis.

Move the line

Slope from two points

Change in y divided by change in x. Subtract in the same order top and bottom.

Slope through (1, 2) and (3, 8)
  1. Change in y: 8 − 2 = 6
  2. Change in x: 3 − 1 = 2
  3. Divide: 6 ÷ 2 = 3

Positive slope goes uphill left to right. Negative goes downhill. Zero is flat.

Writing the equation of a line

You need the slope and one point. Plug the point into y = mx + b to find b.

Line through (1, 2) and (3, 8)
  1. Slope from above: m = 3, so y = 3x + b
  2. Plug in (1, 2): 2 = 3(1) + b
  3. Solve: b = −1
  4. Equation: y = 3x − 1

Lines written as ax + by = c

Get y alone to see the slope. To find where the line crosses an axis, set the other letter to 0.

Slope and x-intercept of 6x + 2y = 12
  1. Move the x term: 2y = −6x + 12
  2. Divide by 2: y = −3x + 6. The slope is −3
  3. For the x-intercept set y = 0: 6x = 12, so x = 2

Parallel and perpendicular

  • Parallel lines have the same slope.
  • Perpendicular lines have slopes that are flipped with the opposite sign. If one slope is 2, the other is −1/2.

Lines in real life

The slope is the rate (per month, per hour, per item) and b is the starting amount. A kayak rental that costs $50 up front plus $30 an hour is y = 30x + 50.

Lesson 8 · PERT algebra

Systems of equations

A system is two equations with the same two unknowns. The solution is the one pair (x, y) that makes both true, which is the point where the two lines cross.

Where do they cross?

Method 1: substitution

Best when one equation already has a letter alone.

y = x + 2 and x + y = 10
  1. Replace y with x + 2: x + (x + 2) = 10
  2. Simplify: 2x + 2 = 10, so x = 4
  3. Put x back in: y = 4 + 2 = 6
  4. Solution: (4, 6)

Method 2: elimination

Add the equations so one letter cancels.

2x + 3y = 12 and 4x − 3y = 6
  1. The y terms are opposites. Add the equations: 6x = 18
  2. x = 3
  3. Put x into the first equation: 2(3) + 3y = 12, so y = 2

If nothing cancels, multiply one equation first.

x + 2y = 7 and 3x − y = 7
  1. Multiply the second equation by 2: 6x − 2y = 14
  2. Now add it to the first: 7x = 21, so x = 3
  3. Put x back in: 3 + 2y = 7, so y = 2

Word problems

A snorkel tour costs $8 for adults and $5 for kids. 20 people pay $130. How many adults?
  1. Two facts make two equations: a + c = 20 and 8a + 5c = 130
  2. From the first, c = 20 − a. Substitute: 8a + 5(20 − a) = 130
  3. Simplify: 3a + 100 = 130
  4. a = 10 adults

Exponents, roots and scientific notation

An exponent is repeated multiplication: x³ means x · x · x. A handful of rules cover nearly every exponent question.

SituationRuleExample
MultiplyingAdd the exponentsx² · x³ = x⁵
DividingSubtract the exponentsx⁶ ÷ x² = x⁴
Power of a powerMultiply the exponents(x²)³ = x⁶
Zero exponentAlways equals 1x⁰ = 1
Negative exponentFlip it to the bottomx⁻² = 1/x²
Fraction exponentBottom is the root, top is the power82/3 = 4

When there are numbers in front

Handle the numbers and the exponents separately.

(3x²)(4x⁵)
  1. Multiply the numbers: 3 × 4 = 12
  2. Add the exponents: 2 + 5 = 7
  3. Answer: 12x⁷
(2x³)²
  1. The power applies to the 2: 2² = 4
  2. And to the x³: 3 × 2 = 6
  3. Answer: 4x⁶

Watch out. Add exponents only when multiplying the same letter. x² + x³ cannot be combined at all.

Square roots

A square root undoes squaring: √25 = 5. To simplify a root that is not perfect, pull out the biggest perfect square inside it.

Simplify √50
  1. Find a perfect square that divides 50: 50 = 25 × 2
  2. Split it: √25 × √2
  3. Answer: 5√2

Roots multiply together: √12 × √3 = √36 = 6.

Fraction exponents

82/3
  1. The bottom, 3, means cube root: the cube root of 8 is 2
  2. The top, 2, means square it: 2² = 4

A way to write very big or very small numbers: one digit, a decimal point, then a power of ten. The power counts how many places the decimal point moved.

The Pythagorean theorem

In a right triangle, the two short sides (legs) and the long side across from the square corner (hypotenuse) are tied together.

Hypotenuse 13, one leg 5. Find the other leg.
  1. 5² + b² = 13²
  2. 25 + b² = 169, so b² = 144
  3. Square root: b = 12

Common whole-number sets worth knowing: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, and their multiples like 6-8-10.

Similar triangles

Similar shapes are the same shape at different sizes, so matching sides have the same ratio.

Small triangle sides 4 and 6. Large triangle matching sides 10 and x.
  1. Scale factor: 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5
  2. Apply it: x = 6 × 2.5 = 15

Geometry on the coordinate grid

  • Midpoint: average the x values and average the y values. Between (2, 1) and (8, 5) it is (5, 3).
  • Distance: the Pythagorean theorem. From (1, 2) to (4, 6) is 3 across and 4 up, so √(9 + 16) = 5.
  • Circle equation: (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r² has center (h, k) and radius r.
Center and radius of (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25
  1. The signs inside flip: the center is (3, −2)
  2. The right side is r²: r = √25 = 5

Lesson 10 · PERT algebra

Polynomials and factoring

A polynomial is several terms added together, like x² + 7x + 10. Multiplying them out and factoring them back are opposite skills, and the test asks for both.

Adding and subtracting

Combine like terms. When subtracting, the minus flips every sign in the second group.

(5x² + 3x − 2) − (2x² − 4x + 6)
  1. Flip the signs of the second group: −2x² + 4x − 6
  2. x² terms: 5x² − 2x² = 3x²
  3. x terms: 3x + 4x = 7x
  4. Numbers: −2 − 6 = −8, giving 3x² + 7x − 8

Multiplying with FOIL

To multiply two parentheses, multiply every pair once: First, Outer, Inner, Last.

See each pair

Watch out. (x + 3)² is (x + 3)(x + 3) = x² + 6x + 9. It is not x² + 9. The middle term is the one people forget.

Factoring: a checklist

  1. Pull out anything common to every term first. 2x² + 10x + 12 = 2(x² + 5x + 6)
  2. Two terms, both squares, minus between: difference of squares. x² − 9 = (x + 3)(x − 3)
  3. Three terms: find two numbers that multiply to the last number and add to the middle number.

Find the pair

The signs tell you what to look for

Last numberMiddle numberThe two numbers are
PositivePositiveBoth positive
PositiveNegativeBoth negative
NegativeEitherOne positive, one negative

When x² has a number in front

If nothing pulls out, split the middle term and factor in two halves.

Factor 2x² + 7x + 3
  1. Multiply the first and last numbers: 2 × 3 = 6
  2. Find two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to 7: 6 and 1
  3. Split the middle term: 2x² + 6x + x + 3
  4. Factor each half: 2x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3)
  5. Answer: (2x + 1)(x + 3)

Test tip. You can always check a factoring answer by FOILing it back out. On multiple choice, FOIL the choices.

Polynomial equations

The same idea as quadratics, with more pieces: get 0 on one side, factor completely, and set every piece equal to 0.

Solve x³ − 9x = 0
  1. Every term has an x. Pull it out: x(x² − 9) = 0
  2. Difference of squares: x(x + 3)(x − 3) = 0
  3. Set each piece to zero: x = 0, x = −3 or x = 3
  • The biggest exponent is the degree. A polynomial can cross the x-axis at most that many times.
  • Far to the right, the term with the biggest exponent wins. If its number is positive the graph rises, and if negative it falls.
Lesson 11 · PERT algebra

Quadratic equations

A quadratic has an x² in it and usually has two answers. Its graph is a U shape called a parabola, and the answers are where the U crosses the x-axis.

Shape the U

Method 1: factor it

Get 0 on one side, factor, then set each parenthesis equal to 0. If two things multiply to zero, one of them has to be zero.

x² + 2x = 15
  1. Get 0 on one side: x² + 2x − 15 = 0
  2. Factor: (x + 5)(x − 3) = 0
  3. Set each to zero: x + 5 = 0 or x − 3 = 0
  4. Answers: x = −5 or x = 3

Method 2: the quadratic formula

Works on every quadratic written as ax² + bx + c = 0, even ones that will not factor.

x = −b ± √b² − 4ac2a

2x² + 5x − 3 = 0, so a = 2, b = 5, c = −3
  1. Under the root: 5² − 4(2)(−3) = 25 + 24 = 49
  2. Square root of 49 is 7
  3. Plug in: x = (−5 ± 7) ÷ 4
  4. Plus gives 2 ÷ 4 = 1/2. Minus gives −12 ÷ 4 = −3

How many solutions?

The part under the root, b² − 4ac, is called the discriminant. Its sign answers the question without solving.

NegativeNoneNever reaches it

The vertex

The vertex is the lowest point of a U that opens up, or the highest point of one that opens down. Its x value is x = −b ÷ (2a). In the form y = a(x − h)² + k the vertex is simply (h, k).

In Rocket League a ball pops up with height h = −16t² + 64t. When does it land, and when is it highest?
  1. It lands when h = 0: −16t² + 64t = 0
  2. Factor out t: t(−16t + 64) = 0, so t = 0 or t = 4. It lands at 4 seconds.
  3. Highest at the vertex: t = −64 ÷ (2 × −16) = 2 seconds
  4. Height then: −16(4) + 64(2) = 64 feet

Where a line meets a parabola

Set the two equations equal to each other and solve the quadratic you get.

Where do y = x² and y = x + 2 cross?
  1. Both equal y, so set them equal: x² = x + 2
  2. Get 0 on one side: x² − x − 2 = 0
  3. Factor: (x − 2)(x + 1) = 0
  4. They cross at: x = 2 or x = −1
Lesson 13 · PERT algebra

Functions

A function is a machine: a number goes in, a number comes out. f(x) is the name of what comes out, read "f of x." It does not mean f times x.

Run the machine

→→

Plugging in

Whatever is inside the parentheses replaces every x, even if it is an expression.

f(x) = 3x − 2. Find f(a + 1).
  1. Replace x with (a + 1): 3(a + 1) − 2
  2. Distribute: 3a + 3 − 2
  3. Answer: 3a + 1

f(x) is another name for y. Saying f(3) = 7 means the point (3, 7) is on the graph.

Domain and range

The domain is every number allowed in. The range is every number that can come out. Two things are never allowed:

  • Dividing by zero. For f(x) = 1/(x − 2), x cannot be 2.
  • The square root of a negative. For f(x) = √(x − 5), x must be 5 or more.

A function inside a function

Work from the inside out.

f(x) = x + 4 and g(x) = 3x. Find f(g(2)).
  1. Inside first: g(2) = 3 · 2 = 6
  2. Feed that to f: f(6) = 6 + 4 = 10

Inverse functions

An inverse, written f⁻¹, runs the machine backwards: you give it the output and it returns the input.

f(x) = 2x + 3. Find f⁻¹(11).
  1. Ask: which x gives 11? 2x + 3 = 11
  2. Subtract 3: 2x = 8
  3. x = 4, so f⁻¹(11) = 4

Moving a graph

ChangeWhat the graph doesExample with y = x²

Watch out. Inside changes look backwards. (x − 2) moves the graph right, not left.

Is it a function?

A rule is a function only if each input gives exactly one output. In a list of points, no x value may repeat with a different y. On a graph, no straight up-and-down line may hit the curve twice.

  • {(1, 4), (2, 4), (3, 7)} is a function. Two inputs sharing an output is fine.
  • {(1, 4), (1, 6), (3, 7)} is not. The input 1 gives two different outputs.

For the range of a parabola, find the vertex. y = (x − 2)² + 3 opens up from (2, 3), so its range is y ≥ 3.

Lesson 12 · PERT algebra

Radicals and rational expressions

A radical equation has x under a square root. A rational equation has x on the bottom of a fraction. Both are solved by undoing the thing that is in the way.

Radical equations

Get the root alone, square both sides, solve, and then check.

√(2x + 1) = 5
  1. Square both sides: 2x + 1 = 25
  2. Subtract 1: 2x = 24
  3. x = 12
  4. Check: √(24 + 1) = √25 = 5. It works.

False answers

Squaring can create an answer that does not work in the original equation. Always plug answers back in.

√(x + 3) = x − 3
  1. Square both sides: x + 3 = x² − 6x + 9
  2. Move everything to one side: x² − 7x + 6 = 0
  3. Factor: (x − 1)(x − 6) = 0, so x = 1 or x = 6
  4. Check 6: √9 = 3 and 6 − 3 = 3. It works.
  5. Check 1: √4 = 2 but 1 − 3 = −2. It fails, so the only answer is x = 6

Simplifying fractions with x

Factor the top and bottom, then cancel anything that matches.

(x² − 9)/(x − 3)
  1. Factor the top: (x + 3)(x − 3)
  2. The (x − 3) on top and bottom cancel.
  3. Answer: x + 3

Watch out. You can cancel whole factors (things multiplied), never single terms. In (x + 3)/3 the 3s do not cancel.

Rational equations

Multiply both sides by the bottom to clear it. When it is one fraction equal to another, cross-multiply.

(x + 1)/(x + 4) = 2/3
  1. Cross-multiply: 3(x + 1) = 2(x + 4)
  2. Distribute: 3x + 3 = 2x + 8
  3. Solve: x = 5

To add fractions with x, use a common bottom just like with numbers: x/2 + x/3 = 3x/6 + 2x/6 = 5x/6.

One page

Formula sheet

Every rule the PERT math test leans on, in one place. Read it the night before, or print it and stick it on the fridge.

Order of operations

Parentheses, Exponents, then Multiply and Divide left to right, then Add and Subtract left to right.

24 ÷ 4 × 2 = 12

Negatives

  • Minus a negative is plus: 5 − (−3) = 8
  • Same signs multiply to positive, different signs to negative
  • (−2)² = 4 but −2² = −4

Fractions

  • Add or subtract: match the bottoms first
  • Multiply: straight across
  • Divide: flip the second, then multiply
  • Mixed number: 2 1/3 = 7/3

Percents

  • part = percent × whole
  • Percent change = change ÷ original
  • 25% off means you pay 75%
  • 1/4 = 0.25 = 25%, 1/8 = 12.5%

Ratios and proportions

  • Cross-multiply: a/b = c/d means ad = bc
  • Unit rate: divide to get the amount for one
  • Smaller unit, multiply. Bigger unit, divide.

Solving equations

  1. Distribute
  2. Combine like terms
  3. x terms on one side, numbers on the other
  4. Divide by the number on x

Flip an inequality sign when you multiply or divide by a negative.

Lines

  • y = mx + b: m is the slope, b is where it crosses the y-axis
  • Slope = change in y ÷ change in x
  • Parallel: same slope. Perpendicular: flip it and change the sign
  • x-intercept: set y = 0

Systems

  • Substitution: plug one equation into the other
  • Elimination: add the equations so a letter cancels
  • Same slope, different b: no solution

Exponents

  • Multiply: add exponents. Divide: subtract
  • Power of a power: multiply
  • x⁰ = 1 and x⁻² = 1/x²
  • √50 = √25 · √2 = 5√2

Polynomials and factoring

  • FOIL: First, Outer, Inner, Last
  • (x + a)² = x² + 2ax + a²
  • x² − a² = (x + a)(x − a)
  • Trinomial: two numbers that multiply to the last and add to the middle
  • Always pull out a common factor first

Quadratics

  • Get 0 on one side, factor, set each part to 0
  • x = −b ± √b² − 4ac2a
  • b² − 4ac positive: two answers, zero: one, negative: none
  • Vertex at x = −b ÷ 2a

Radicals and fractions with x

  • Square both sides, then check every answer
  • Cancel whole factors only, never single terms
  • The bottom of a fraction can never be 0
  • No square roots of negatives

Functions

  • f(3): put 3 in for every x
  • f(g(x)): work inside out
  • Inverse: run it backwards

Geometry

  • Rectangle: A = lw, P = 2l + 2w
  • Triangle: A = ½bh, angles add to 180°
  • Circle: A = πr², C = 2πr
  • Right triangle: a² + b² = c²

Words into math

  • of, times, per: multiply
  • less than: subtract, and it flips the order. "5 less than x" is x − 5
  • is, equals: =
  • sum, total: add
Before you go

Test day guide

Knowing exactly how the PERT works removes most of the surprises. Here is what to expect and how to use it to your advantage.

How the PERT math test works

  • 30 multiple-choice questions. 25 count toward your score. The other 5 are trial questions that do not count, and you cannot tell which ones they are, so give every question your best.
  • No time limit. Take as long as you need. Most people finish the math part in under an hour.
  • Nobody passes or fails. You get a score from 50 to 150, and it only decides which math class you start in.
  • It adapts to you. A right answer brings a harder question next, so hard questions mean it is going well.
  • You cannot skip or go back. Each answer is final once you confirm it.

What you can and cannot bring

  • No calculator of your own. An on-screen calculator pops up on some questions only. Practise without one.
  • Scratch paper is provided. Use it for every problem. Writing each step down frees your head for the thinking.
  • Bring a photo ID. Check with the testing center for anything else they need.

What score do you need?

In Florida, 114 is the college-ready math score and usually places you into Intermediate Algebra (MAT 1033). 123 or higher usually places you into College Algebra (MAC 1105). Each college publishes its own placement chart, so ask yours which class you are aiming for, then set that score as your goal on your profile.

On each question

  1. Read the last line first. Know what it is asking for before you start calculating.
  2. Work it on paper, then find your answer among the choices.
  3. Stuck? Use the choices. Plug each one into the problem and see which works, or cross out the ones that cannot be right.
  4. Check before you confirm. Re-read the question and look for a sign slip. You cannot come back to it.
  5. Then let it go. One hard question does not decide your placement.

The week before

  • Do a short session here each day instead of one long cram.
  • Spend most of the time on lessons marked Learning or Proficient. Polishing what you half-know pays off more than starting brand-new topics.
  • Take a PERT practice test every few days and watch the score move.
  • Ask the testing center about its retake rules. They differ from college to college.

If your heart is racing

That is your body sending extra fuel to your brain. It is a sign you are ready, and it does not mean you are about to do badly. If worries are crowding your head on the morning of the test, some people find it helps to spend five minutes writing them down on paper first, to get them out of the way.

A plan for getting stuck. Decide now: "If I am stuck for a few minutes, I will try the answer choices, pick the best one, and move on." Having the plan ready means you do not have to make that decision under pressure.
